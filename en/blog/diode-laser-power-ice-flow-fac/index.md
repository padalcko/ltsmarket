## A good laser is more than a wattage figure

When beauty professionals compare diode laser systems, power is often one of the first specifications they consider.

A handpiece rated at 2000 W, 3000 W or 4000 W may sound impressive. However, the number alone tells us surprisingly little about how a device performs during treatment.

What matters is how accurately the system delivers optical energy, how consistently it maintains the selected settings and how effectively it manages heat.

A modern diode laser combines several closely connected technologies.

The laser diodes generate light. Optical components guide that light towards the treatment area. Electronics control the pulses. Cooling systems manage temperature.

To understand the quality of a laser, we need to look at all these systems together.

Using Lumos as an example, let us examine three important technologies.

## 1. ICE Flow. Understanding laser cooling

Laser hair removal works by delivering light energy that is absorbed by melanin.

Part of this energy is converted into heat, which can affect structures associated with the hair follicle.

This process is based on selective photothermolysis.

However, melanin is also present in the epidermis. Excessive heating of the skin surface may therefore cause unwanted thermal effects.

Cooling helps manage this risk.

### What exactly is ICE Flow?

ICE Flow is the name used for the cooling system associated with Lumos.

It should not be confused with a universal technical standard.

Modern laser cooling systems may incorporate circulating coolant, heat exchangers, temperature sensors, thermoelectric components and cooled contact surfaces.

The precise internal configuration of ICE Flow must be verified against the manufacturer's technical documentation.

Its essential purpose is temperature management during operation.

### Why does temperature stability matter?

A cold treatment window does not automatically mean that a cooling system performs well during prolonged use.

Consider two laser handpieces.

Both feel cold before treatment begins.

The first maintains a stable temperature during repeated pulses.

The second gradually becomes warmer because its cooling system cannot remove heat quickly enough.

The difference becomes apparent under sustained operating conditions.

This is why professionals should consider cooling capacity and thermal stability rather than focusing only on the lowest advertised contact temperature.

### Does better cooling make higher energy automatically safe?

No.

Epidermal cooling can improve treatment tolerance and reduce certain thermal risks.

However, safe parameter selection still depends on skin phototype, tanning, hair characteristics, wavelength, pulse duration and device design.

Cooling supports safe operation. It does not replace professional assessment or appropriate treatment protocols.

## 2. Why does a 2000–4000 W power range matter in 2026 and 2027?

Lumos is offered in configurations with a declared handpiece power range of 2000 to 4000 W.

Its published specifications include pulse energy from 10 to 240 J, repetition rates from 1 to 30 Hz and pulse durations starting at 5 ms.

These figures describe a potentially flexible operating range.

However, understanding the units is essential.

### Watts, joules and fluence

Watts measure power, or the rate at which energy is transferred.

Joules measure energy.

Fluence describes the energy delivered per unit area and is commonly expressed in J/cm².

Two devices with the same advertised wattage may therefore deliver very different treatment characteristics.

Spot size, optical output, pulse duration, wavelength and beam distribution all matter.

It is also important to establish what a manufacturer means by handpiece power.

A specification may refer to optical output, peak power, electrical input or a combined nominal rating of laser emitters.

These values are not interchangeable.

### What is power headroom?

Power headroom means having additional available capacity beyond what is required for a particular operating condition.

In principle, this can provide greater flexibility when generating pulses and maintaining selected output levels.

However, a larger power reserve is only useful when the optical, electronic and cooling systems can support it.

A powerful laser that cannot maintain its operating temperature may have to limit output during demanding treatment conditions.

The objective is not to operate at maximum power all the time.

The objective is to deliver the required energy accurately and consistently.

### Why are adjustable parameters so important?

No single treatment setting is appropriate for every client.

Skin phototype, hair thickness, pigmentation, treatment area and individual tissue response all influence parameter selection.

Adjustable pulse energy, pulse duration and repetition rate provide flexibility within the device's approved operating protocols.

A shorter pulse delivers its energy over a shorter period.

A longer pulse spreads energy delivery over more time.

The clinical implications depend on the complete treatment configuration, not on pulse duration alone.

### Does 30 Hz guarantee faster treatment?

Not necessarily.

A repetition rate of 30 Hz means that the system can generate 30 pulses per second in a supported operating mode.

It does not tell us how much energy is available at that rate.

For example, delivering 240 J at 30 Hz would require an average optical output of 7200 W if both maximum values were available simultaneously.

This illustrates why published maximum specifications must be interpreted carefully.

Maximum pulse energy and maximum repetition rate may refer to different operating conditions.

Professionals should request the actual combinations of settings supported by the device.

### What matters most in 2026 and 2027?

Beauty clinics increasingly value consistent performance, flexible protocols, reliable operation and access to technical support.

A wide adjustment range and sufficient power capacity can help meet these expectations.

However, there is no universal scientific rule stating that a modern hair removal laser must deliver 4000 W.

Measured performance and appropriate treatment parameters remain more important than the largest number in a brochure.

## 3. FAC. How optical engineering controls laser light

FAC stands for Fast Axis Collimation.

It is an optical technique used to control the divergence of light emitted by laser diodes.

### Why does diode laser light need optical correction?

A laser diode does not emit a perfectly parallel beam.

Light spreads as it leaves the emitting region.

This divergence is generally different along two perpendicular axes.

The direction with greater divergence is known as the fast axis.

Without optical correction, efficiently collecting and transporting the emitted light becomes more difficult.

### How does FAC work?

A specially designed optical element, often a small cylindrical microlens, is positioned close to the laser emitter.

Its purpose is to reduce divergence along the fast axis.

This process is called collimation.

By controlling the direction of the emitted light, the optical system can guide it more effectively through subsequent components.

FAC does not generate additional laser energy.

It helps manage the energy that the diodes have already produced.

### Why is FAC important in high power diode systems?

High power diode modules may contain multiple emitters.

Each emitter produces light that must be collected and guided through the optical system.

Poor optical alignment or unsuitable beam geometry can reduce the amount of usable light transmitted through the system.

FAC can improve optical collection and facilitate further beam shaping.

It is therefore an important part of the engineering behind many high power diode laser assemblies.

### Does FAC guarantee better hair removal results?

No.

FAC is an optical engineering technique, not a clinical outcome guarantee.

Uniform energy distribution at the treatment window depends on the entire optical design.

Other lenses, beam shaping components, homogenisation optics and the treatment window also influence the final beam profile.

FAC should not be confused with a complete beam homogenisation system.

Actual optical measurements are needed to evaluate energy distribution and transmission efficiency.

## 4. Why do cooling, power and optics belong together?

These three technologies perform different functions, but their performance is interconnected.

A powerful laser source places greater demands on thermal management.

The optical system determines how effectively emitted light is transmitted towards the treatment window.

Electronics regulate pulse generation and output settings.

Cooling helps maintain suitable operating temperatures and contributes to epidermal protection.

The quality of a diode laser depends on how successfully these components work together.

This is why comparing devices solely by advertised handpiece wattage is insufficient.
