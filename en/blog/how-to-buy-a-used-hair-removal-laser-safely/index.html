How to Buy a Used Hair Removal Laser Without Getting Scammed: Shot Counters, Counterfeits and Hidden Faults

26.11.2026 · Dmytro Zaitsev — CEO, founder and lead technical specialist

Buying a used hair removal laser does not require you to be an engineer, understand electronics or know how a laser handpiece is built. One of the most reliable ways to reduce the risk of a bad purchase is to have the equipment assessed by specialists who repair and maintain aesthetic lasers before you hand over your money. However, even when a seller demonstrates a low shot count, original documentation and a functioning device, there are important details every salon owner should understand.

Why buying a used laser is similar to buying a used car

Imagine two cars manufactured in the same year. One has covered 70,000 kilometres, while the other has covered 150,000. Which would you choose?

At first glance, the answer seems obvious. But what if the first car has undergone major repairs, received irregular maintenance and had its mileage altered? Meanwhile, the second has a complete service history and has been maintained regularly.

The situation with aesthetic equipment is remarkably similar.

An attractive appearance, a low shot count and convincing assurances from the seller do not necessarily mean that a laser is in good technical condition or has a long remaining service life.

The most important components are located inside the device. Their condition cannot be assessed from photographs in an online listing.

Can you trust a laser shot counter?

The number of pulses is one of the first parameters buyers consider when purchasing used equipment.

Understandably, buyers want to know how intensively a laser has been used and how long it might continue operating.

However, shot counters have limitations.

Depending on the design of the equipment, pulse count information may be stored in the control system, handpiece electronics or other components. During repairs or replacement of electronic boards or handpieces, the displayed count may change or be reset.

Not every counter reset indicates fraud. Sometimes it is a legitimate consequence of a repair. The problem arises when buyers are not informed about such interventions or are deliberately misled about the equipment's history.

Even if the shot counter displays an accurate number, another question remains: under what conditions was the laser operated?

Research into semiconductor laser degradation indicates that service life is influenced by factors including:

  • laser diode temperature;
  • operating electrical current;
  • heat dissipation efficiency;
  • optical output power;
  • electrical overload;
  • moisture exposure and component damage.

Two identical laser devices with the same shot count may be in completely different technical conditions.

A shot counter should therefore be treated as an additional source of information rather than a guarantee of remaining service life.

The laser works, but does it deliver the required power?

The seller switches on the device. The display works, the cooling system starts and the handpiece generates pulses. Everything appears to be functioning correctly.

But do the actual laser emission parameters correspond to the values displayed on the screen?

A laser may generate pulses while delivering reduced or unstable output. Possible causes include diode module degradation, damaged optics, power supply problems or insufficient cooling.

Without suitable measurement equipment, these issues can be difficult to identify.

During a professional inspection, a specialist uses measuring instruments selected according to the laser type, wavelength and emission characteristics. The results are compared with the technical documentation for the specific model.

Output stability during operation is also important.

A device that performs successfully during a short demonstration may begin overheating or become unstable under prolonged operating conditions.

For a salon, this can mean interrupted treatments, dissatisfied clients and temporary business downtime.

Counterfeit lasers: when a well-known brand exists only on the casing

Counterfeiting is not limited to car parts, clothing and consumer electronics. Copies of well-known brands also exist in the professional aesthetic equipment market.

Counterfeit aesthetic devices have been discussed in scientific journals including Lasers in Surgery and Medicine, Aesthetic Surgery Journal and the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology.

A convincing imitation may have a similar casing, user interface, handpiece and logo. This does not mean that it contains the same components, protective systems or radiation sources.

It is important to distinguish counterfeit equipment from affordable devices manufactured by other companies. A less familiar or budget-oriented brand is not necessarily inferior. The problem arises when one device is falsely represented as another or misleading information is provided about its origin.

Before purchasing a branded laser, consider checking:

  • the device serial number;
  • handpiece serial numbers, where applicable;
  • the manufacturer's identification plate;
  • whether the documentation matches the specific model;
  • the equipment's origin;
  • whether authenticity can be confirmed by the manufacturer.

If a seller cannot explain where the equipment came from, it is advisable not to rush into the purchase.

Hidden faults that cannot be seen in photographs

Preparing equipment for resale may involve cleaning the casing, replacing external parts and correcting cosmetic damage.

This is normal practice, but the appearance of a device does not reveal the condition of its internal components.

A professional technical inspection may identify problems such as:

  • reduced laser module output;
  • evidence of internal coolant leaks;
  • damaged optical components;
  • insufficient coolant circulation;
  • faulty fans or pumps;
  • damaged handpiece cables;
  • unstable power supply operation;
  • consequences of improper repairs;
  • faulty temperature monitoring systems.

A previous repair does not automatically make a device a poor choice.

A professionally repaired laser with documented component replacements and technical test results may be a better investment than equipment with an unknown operating history.

The important question is not whether the laser has been repaired, but what was repaired, who performed the work and what results were obtained afterwards.

Is CE marking enough to ensure a safe purchase?

When purchasing aesthetic equipment in Poland, European conformity requirements must be considered.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/2346 establishes common specifications for certain categories of products without an intended medical purpose, including laser and IPL equipment used for hair removal and other skin treatments.

For professional equipment covered by the relevant provisions, regular output monitoring is required, including measurement of light energy density at least annually in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

However, CE marking does not confirm the current technical condition of a used device.

Equipment may have complied with applicable requirements when it was placed on the market but subsequently suffered damage, improper repairs or modifications.

Documentation verification and technical inspection therefore complement each other.

Frequently asked questions

Can a shot counter determine the remaining service life of a laser?

No. A shot counter helps assess operating history but does not account for every factor contributing to wear. Technical diagnostics are required to assess the current condition. Even a professional inspection cannot guarantee an exact number of future pulses.

Can an altered laser shot counter be detected?

In some cases, discrepancies can be identified by reviewing service records, available system data and component replacement history. Recovering the actual historical shot count is not always possible.

Is it worth buying a repaired laser?

Yes, provided that repairs were performed professionally, the repair history is documented, and output parameters and safety systems have been checked.
